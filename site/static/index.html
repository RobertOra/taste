How good is your music taste?

Do the artists you discover go on to blow up? We compare the music you got into with what happened to it next, against thousands of other listeners.

Try it

  1. Get your Spotify history. In Spotify, go to Account → Privacy, tick Extended streaming history and request it. Spotify emails it to you (it can take a few days, sometimes a few weeks). The quicker one-year export won't work: each find needs a year to see what happened next.
  2. Drop the files here. The .zip Spotify sends, or the .json files inside it.
  3. See your score. Takes about half a minute.

You need at least two years of history and a hundred or so artists or songs you really got into.

How this works

A find is an artist (or song) you played in at least four separate weeks within six months of first hearing it. We skip your first six months of history, when everything is new.

For each find, we check how many new listeners it gained over the next year on ListenBrainz, compared with artists that were exactly as popular when you found them, the same month, in the same corner of music. Your score is how your finds did on average, over your last five years of judged finds, ranked against about 15,000 ListenBrainz listeners scored the same way.

Why a range? Any one find is mostly luck, so a score built from a few hundred of them is fuzzy. The range is where your true rank most likely sits (80% sure). More history narrows it.

Based on a study of whether good taste is real, using 600,000 Last.fm listeners and checked again on ListenBrainz: the same people keep picking well, and it still works on today's listening. Taste also drifts, so we look at your recent years.
